1. What this webinar was

An information session, not a negotiation. Three UN agencies presented a new support offer for countries that want to test the Beyond GDP dashboard. No member state spoke. The audience typed questions into the Zoom Q&A, and the moderator read them out.

Why it matters. The UN now runs a country track next to the General Assembly process. Countries can join it before any resolution.

The Deputy Secretary-General, Amina J. Mohammed, opened by video. Anu Peltola (UNCTAD) hosted. Daniel Hopp (UNCTAD) presented the dashboard and moderated the questions. Heriberto Tapia (UNDP) presented the policy side and the next steps. Shantanu Mukherjee (UN DESA) spoke on the statistical and intergovernmental track.

UNCTAD UNDP UN DESA Deputy Secretary-General

The UN held the same session twice for different time zones. This readout covers the first session. The second session ran on 22 October at 07:00 UTC with the same programme.

2. Executive summary

Five months after Counting What Counts, the UN moves part of the work from New York to the countries. UNCTAD, UNDP and DESA offer pilot support, an online hub and a peer-learning network. The intergovernmental process continues in parallel, but the speakers gave no date for its next step.

  • A country track starts now. The offer is voluntary. Pilot support goes to a limited number of countries. Every country can use the hub.
  • The next deadline is 30 November 2026. Countries send an expression of interest by then. Focus pilots start in December. The hub launches in December or January.
  • The General Assembly track waits for co-facilitators. DESA says the President of the General Assembly has not yet appointed them for this session. The elements paper is out.
  • There is no reporting obligation and no global data release. The UN calls the dashboard a proposal for country testing. It holds back country data so that countries can check their own figures first.
  • The headline-index question stays open. The elements paper lists three options and picks none. The pilots can give evidence for the choice.
3. What is new since the July panel

Each item is checked against the three earlier Beyond GDP readouts. New means the earlier readouts do not carry it. Update means it changes an earlier item. Through-line means it continues an earlier theme.

ItemStatusWhat was saidWhy it matters
Country track New The Deputy Secretary-General describes a voluntary platform, led by UNCTAD and UNDP. It offers pilot support to a limited number of countries, a community of practice and an online repository (full quote below). Work at country level starts before a General Assembly decision.
Dates New The expression-of-interest form opened at 15:30 UTC on 21 October. The first deadline is 30 November 2026. Later forms are taken on a rolling basis. Focus pilots start in December 2026. The hub launch is marked for December 2026 to January 2027. Tapia says the pilots scale up in 2027. This is the next deadline after the 10 August elements-paper input.
Who does what New UNCTAD: statistical guidance, compilation tools and the hub. UNDP: policy applications and country case studies. UN DESA: global follow-up and the Statistical Commission. A slide lists the donors as “China, 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development Sub-Fund of the UN Peace and Development Fund”. Readers know whom to contact for data, for policy and for the global process.
State of the GA process Update Tapia says the elements paper came out “just a few weeks ago”, from “way more than 100 contributions”. Mukherjee says the co-facilitators for this session are not yet appointed (full quote below). Peltola says member states decide the next steps, and she is unsure how far this session gets (full quote below). In July, Spain and Guyana co-facilitated and the input call was open. The process now sits between two co-facilitation terms. The aim is still a GA resolution.
Headline index Update A UNDP slide: “The Elements Paper records the options — none, a few, a single aggregate — without settling them.” Tapia says the pilots can show how countries use composite indicators. Mukherjee asks how valid a composite is for its intended use. In July the question was raised. It is now written into the negotiating text as three options, and country pilots become an evidence source.
What member states ask for New A UNDP slide sums up the consultations in four asks. Coherence: “Dock into the existing data ecosystem (SDGs, MVI, HDI, SNA, SEEA)”, with the Statistical Commission as the technical venue. Ownership: “A common, comparable core with a flexible national periphery: country-owned additions, not modifications. The point with the broadest agreement.” Policy relevance: planning, budgets, finance allocation. Capacity: “Sequencing and support, not lower ambition.” This is the first UN summary of the consultations. It names national ownership as the widest common ground, which matches the May consultation.
No reporting obligation, no global data yet New Hopp calls the dashboard a proposal for country testing, not a universal reporting obligation (full quote below). Peltola says the UN reviewed data availability but does not publish country data yet. There is no intergovernmental decision on when reporting starts (full quote below). This speaks to the concern, raised in May, that the metrics could be imposed on countries.
The data behind the 31 indicators New Hopp shows coverage per indicator, from public international sources as of April 2026. He calls the figures “preliminary and indicative”. Examples:
  • Violence against women: data for one year only, 2018.
  • Biodiversity Intactness Index: no new figure since 2014.
  • Household disposable income: “only 70 to 80 countries” report it to UNSD.
  • Wealth share of the top 1%: “only about 30% of countries” have survey or wealth-account figures. The rest is modelled.
  • Life satisfaction and loneliness: mainly from the Gallup World Poll. The HLEG wants country-led surveys.
A slide says coverage is lowest for sustainability and resilience, at about 1 in 3 developing countries. Several indicators lag 2 to 4 years.		 These figures put numbers on the capacity concern that delegations raised in March and May. Hopp says countries can start with a small set of indicators (full quote below).
A 2027 target New Hopp recalls that the HLEG report asks governments to “establish national progress measuring initiatives by 2027”. The earlier readouts do not carry this date. It is in the report, and the pilots run inside that window.
Finance link Through-line No speaker discussed the finance link head-on. It appears in three places: the Global Alliance, which aims to inform “development financing considerations”; the “finance allocation” line on the member-state slide; and Tapia’s point that the use of pilot results for international cooperation is “an open question”. The finance link was the main fault line in May. The country track does not settle it, so it stays with the General Assembly process.
Poverty data New Tapia says the latest global Multidimensional Poverty Index update, released “a few days ago”, shows multidimensional poverty “increasing for the first time”. He notes that more than 50 countries use a national MPI. Tapia uses it to show what income figures can miss. Check the HDRO/OPHI release before citing the figure.
Tools and partners New A five-phase self-assessment toolkit; practice examples from Canada (budget proposals), Italy (fiscal planning), Bhutan (policy screening) and Wales (statutory objectives); the Youth Moving Beyond GDP network (Beyond Lab at UN Geneva, UNCTAD, Rethinking Economics International); IISD and OECD as partners. This is what a country gets when it joins.

Repeats from earlier readouts are left out: GDP alone is not enough; 31 indicators in four components, 15 from the SDGs; the history from SDG target 17.19 to the Pact for the Future; the existence of the Global Alliance.

Foundational principles: 5 indicators (3 Tier I, 2 Tier II). Current well-being: 14 (10, 4). Equity and inclusion: 6 (3, 3). Sustainability and resilience: 6 (4, 2). The data gaps per indicator are in section 3. He closes with the HLEG asks to governments, the statistical community and civil society.
Heriberto Tapia
22:34		 Head of Research, Human Development Report Office, UNDP The aim is better policy, not better indicators for their own sake (full quote below). The country pilots are bottom-up and look at seven areas: policy relevance of existing indicators; links to plans, budgets and sector strategies; composite indices; subnational and disaggregated data, designed in from the start; AI and satellite data; people’s own view of their lives (a slide cites “7 in 10 people” who feel they have little influence on government decisions, from HDR 2023/24); and communication, with Beyond GDP figures reported as often and as prominently as GDP figures. The pilots are built so that results can be combined for the global process.
Anu Peltola
42:13		 UNCTAD The Beyond GDP Data for Action and Innovation Hub has three pillars: data (framework, metadata, data availability), policy (country experience, community of practice) and toolkits. Countries can engage as pilots, through a national focal point (usually in the statistics office), through peer learning, or by sharing resources. Pilots do not need to test every indicator. The Global Alliance (Sevilla Platform for Action; Spain, OECD, UNCTAD, SEGIB) and the youth network also feed the hub.
Shantanu Mukherjee
1:14 and 1:18		 Director, UN Statistics Division, UN DESA Measurement changes as new data arrives; the MPI is his example. National measures can serve national policy, but DESA must keep measures comparable across countries (full quote below). The Statistical Commission runs the technical track. The co-facilitators for the GA track are not yet appointed. He names the HLPF and the voluntary national reviews as places to reach a wider audience.
Heriberto Tapia
1:20		 UNDP; next steps The dates in section 8. The self-assessment toolkit has five phases: institutional setup, mapping national agencies, data availability and quality review, data collection and validation, and dissemination. Its results can be combined to inform the intergovernmental process. Academics can join as individuals or as expert groups.
5. Audience questions

Hopp read the questions from the Zoom Q&A in groups. The questions are paraphrased and numbered. Askers are not named. Some answers came from more than one speaker.

#QuestionAnswered byAnswer
Audience question 1 What are the next steps of the intergovernmental process? Peltola Member states decide. An elements paper guides their talks. There is broad support, “but of course, there will be areas for debate.”
Audience question 2 Is there a schedule for the first hub webinars? Can countries present their work? Peltola Yes. The first webinars show country good practice and come “towards the end of this year”. The UN also briefs the resident coordinator offices in all countries.
Audience question 3 Is the hub open to local and regional governments? Peltola Yes. The UN wants indicators that can be localised and disaggregated below country level. It will share subnational practice on the hub.
Audience question 4 How can organisations inside or outside the UN add their tools to the hub? Peltola Write to beyondgdp@un.org. The team reviews the tool and shares it.
Audience question 5 Should governments set up teams? Which departments should take part? Peltola Form a cross-agency team. Each country decides who joins, because mandates differ. The national statistics office is usually well placed to coordinate.
Audience question 6 Is there a global dashboard with the existing data? Peltola No. The UN checked data availability. It gives countries time to review their own data before it shares any data globally. Countries can start compiling now.
Audience question 7 What if hard data improves but people say life is worse? Which signal guides policy? Tapia The dashboard exists to examine such gaps. Sometimes the objective indicator misses what people feel. Sometimes the perception is wrong and people need better information. Either way, perceptions “inform their own action”, so they count. The pilots will test this.
Audience question 8 Should a composite index use equal weights or national weights? Tapia, Mukherjee Tapia: still open; there is a trade-off between richness and ease of communication (full quote below); “stay tuned”. Mukherjee: decide first what the composite is for, then test its validity for that use.
Audience question 9 How is this not a duplicate of the SDGs, the Better Life Index and other dashboards? Tapia, Peltola, Mukherjee Tapia: member states ask for coherence, and the HLEG built on earlier work. The pilots will show which indicators add value and which “bring additional burden”. Peltola: the framework connects the national accounts, environmental accounts and well-being work. Mukherjee: Statistical Commission expert groups already develop methods for these measures.
6. Where this fits in the Beyond GDP sequence
4 Mar 2026

GA briefing

Interim dashboard presented. Broad, soft support. Capacity already a shared concern.

7 May 2026

Open consultation

27 delegations react to the report. Fault lines on the finance link, subjective indicators and venue.

Jul 2026

HLPF panel

Talk turns to implementation. Spain and Guyana open the elements-paper input call.

21 Oct 2026

Country-phase launch

The UN opens a country track. The GA track waits for new co-facilitators.

The sequence now has two tracks. The General Assembly track decides the political questions, including the finance link and any headline index. The country track tests the indicators in practice and feeds evidence back. Speakers say both tracks inform each other, through the Statistical Commission and the elements paper.
